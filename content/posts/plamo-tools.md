# First model/初めのプラモ

A re-org shuttered the department I worked in last year and while we were safely (mostly) transferred to another division, we had to move down a floor. The upside was we got to choose our own cubicles, and were given a week or so to get comfortable. I had a double-wide workspace cobbled together from two different cubicles and while a colleague and I were rummaging through vacant cubicles, scavenging for shelving, VESA mounts, cables and the like, she located an unopened SD 00 Raiser kit. Who did this belong to? Why was it left behind?

![image.png](/uploads/image-8bda5483-3cf9-44ce-92c4-4f3dcc7717f8.png)
My coworker knew I was a fan of anime, so she gave it to me. I was somewhat familiar with Gundam, having watched Wing back when it was on Toonami, but I had no particular affection for the franchise. It sat unboxed on my desk for two months or so until I was just bored enough to investigate.

# 仕事のお供/Work Mascot

This was probably one of the better grades to have been given. Even an HG would have been too daunting a task, as I hadn't assembled a kit in 30 years or so. It took around a lunch break or so to build the Raiser 00, and he now lives under my monitor (next to the excellent Nothing buds and the GFred).

Even in the photo the mistakes are clear: I twisted the pieces from the runner, I sanded nothing, and even then I knew I hated stickers, so there are numerous details missing.![image.png](/uploads/image-81d4ef30-7e20-4dca-bdad-346c206a675d.png)Even so, it's one of my favorite builds, and I've put together maybe 20 or so kits by this point. (I keep him in his rough state as a reminder of how my skills have progressed). It turns out that not only do I highly enjoy model building, plamo is a quite suitable hobby for a new-ish father with maybe \~90 minutes of free time daily.

# Tools

Since I've leaned into plamo, I've accumulated a decent amount of tools. Some are indispensable, others less so.

## スティック・ガラス・スポンジヤスリなど/Sanding Sticks, sponges, pads, etc

These are a mixed bag. I started with a set of [stedi-brand glass files](https://www.amazon.com/stedi-Polishing-Ultimate-Precision-Resin-Base/dp/B0CFLKZX6B/ref=sr_1_5?crid=IK0Q9TM25HLD&dib=eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.lCPp8o1dXLbO8_4Flh6DJ3qn3BowF000SWMG9EIxRplOXGO_jGn1RUz5IgBGzQR8gHcVGvFq7RaLhRuf-5lWqOmElRrw2e-ALdtrEfA4C2ekS17j5MVVjpyfJKAV5Q6lU1-2c8m87oNepKN1w5Hxb1cIvXdjpgN-ZOyrdAhJJVBQ_HjXv5NEsUao3Px94__iDSKIoCJWevuk3Bt3r7J59er8GXzs_RQRBMlTIdkzCeg.xcr9NpN0YKrGwioy7fUg-ibG3srSnjLnSFByofZAURo&dib_tag=se&keywords=dspiae%2Bglass%2Bfile%2Bset&qid=1794932751&sprefix=dspiae%2Bglas%2Caps%2C246&sr=8-5&th=1) and they were fine for a bit, but I found more common utility in normal sanding sticks and sponges. I still use them on occasion, but I could get by without them. The [stedi sanding sticks](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DM7ZTJJ1?ref_=ppx_hzsearch_conn_dt_b_fed_asin_title_1&th=1) are my go-to (after I blew threw the Infini-brand sticks, which are no longer on Amazon). Great give and grit. If I could find the large Infini sticks, I would purchase some, but I don't think they are in production anymore, or Amazon doesn't carry them. They are on par with the stedi sticks, and either will get you moving in the right direction.

![image.png](/uploads/image-ca2ceb63-dbb4-4473-b181-7ec2eef6efeb.png)

The [stedi carbon fiber sticks](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D1DT3P6W?ref_=ppx_hzsearch_conn_dt_b_fed_asin_title_2&th=1) only see a low-medium amount of use, but they work well when I need them. Only on taking the photo do I realize how often I go for stedi where available. Good brand, good price. The self-adhesive sandpaper sheets are fine, though it seems like they lose grit quickly.

I also have a (surprise) [stedi knife](https://www.amazon.com/stedi-Making-Precision-Blades-Military/dp/B0CXTCV3TH/ref=sr_1_8?crid=39U8KUEGDTCJV&dib=eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.pi-CjFuEDWO59xvHMcvo2D2NNci9xzg4WSuY49cnsXY6RJmUljnUgMIDOW1A8VEH4oU4P0k_SXWiig5qjG60LEtgIUS7jS_p61xlcSIVks0CkFKU3eKsHOhZUIzINbVpYhpJVaqT6ar-B3fXerX3DhoehU3tQ3jqkWqYps3iEJmeqJZ1dKYShoVWKenfn2JAhqWI_Md86TcmLwUiUyibs6WE2RtZ1gY6-gplCHvUliBNnj-WbhuqpL_cpdH5Il4rYcn_JiFX_YkwFn9L9cafaCPPFOimRRJCLY5yhqpcX60.5fK_MKIc9DYZ6JRY4fJ7rahNQm_2j3IIpOdAbXUwoxk&dib_tag=se&keywords=hobby%2Bknife&qid=1794933670&sprefix=hobby%2Bknife%2Caps%2C193&sr=8-8&th=1), and it's fine. They are both fine. It's a hobby knife, they have to be pretty bad to not rise to the level of "fine."
![image.png](/uploads/image-5b20ce45-a136-4e5b-b86f-8332fdcbcec9.png)
The real difference lies in the blades you use: The stedi knife uses the 45-degree blades the kit came with, and the Tamiya knife is for typical No. 11 hobby blades. I use the stedi knife for removing flashing and scraping away mold lines, and the Tamiya for cutting down proud nubs.

## スジボリタガネ/scriber

stedi again: I (poorly) use the [stedi blue handle with a 0.15mm chisel](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CMT3R3RV?ref_=ppx_hzsearch_conn_dt_b_fed_asin_title_5&th=1). It's fantastic, though I wish the handle was a bit smaller. I originally wanted a BMC tagane scriber, though I am glad I opted for the stedi since the BMCs are woefully expensive and my skill woudln't justify such an outrageous purchase. Not much to say about this, since I don't use it too often.
![image.png](/uploads/image-33c6fa91-a8c0-4180-8482-98d6b3307020.png)
What I do use is the scriber I got from Daiso. It's unidirectional, being a needle point rather than a chisel, and it's more forgiving as it's less sharp. It also creates a V-shaped channel, so its's good for clean lines. It also works well to get panel lines ready to better accept the stedi scriber, since it tends to stay where I want it to more when the panel lines are deeper/better defined.

![image.png](/uploads/image-ff52105b-3656-423b-afd0-cc99b3675160.png)

## パーツセパレーター/Part Separator

I bought this on a whim — it's just one of those things where I say, "That's so wasteful. When will I use this? I have other tools," but when I do need to use it, I'm glad I own it. It's kind on plastic and has a protrusion making it easy to poke pieces out from their undersides, which has been a blessing more than once. Every manufacturer under the sun makes a variation on this tool, and I've seen plenty with metal blades, though I'd probably stay clear of those. Worth the $5.
![image.png](/uploads/image-6137a147-2762-4e9b-afc5-5eb7cd317fe5.png)
